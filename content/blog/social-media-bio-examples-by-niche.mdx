A good social media bio isn't a personality quiz answer — it's a filter and a
pitch at the same time. It needs to tell the right person, in about two
seconds, that this account is for them specifically. What that looks like
changes a lot depending on your niche.

Here are nine examples across common niches, with notes on the structure
behind each one.

## 1. Freelancer / service provider

> Copywriter for SaaS landing pages. Helped 40+ companies rewrite their
> homepage without a redesign. DM for availability →

**What it's doing:** Leads with the specific service, not "writer." The
number (40+) functions as social proof without a testimonials page. The CTA
tells visitors exactly what action to take.

## 2. Content creator / educator

> I teach personal finance without the jargon. New video every Tuesday.
> Free budget template ↓

**What it's doing:** States the value proposition and the format (weekly
video) so visitors know what following actually gets them. The lead magnet
link gives a reason to act immediately instead of just following and
forgetting.

## 3. Small business owner

> Family-run coffee roastery in Portland since 2019. Small-batch, direct
> trade. Order online or find us at the Saturday market.

**What it's doing:** Establishes credibility (family-run, since 2019) and
differentiator (small-batch, direct trade) in one line. Gives two concrete
ways to convert — online and in person — instead of a vague "shop now."

## 4. Job seeker / career-focused profile

> Product manager, B2B SaaS. Currently between roles and open to opportunities
> in fintech or healthtech. Portfolio and case studies below ↓

**What it's doing:** States role and industry specificity instead of "looking
for opportunities." Being direct about "between roles" removes ambiguity for
recruiters scanning quickly. The portfolio link gives an immediate next step.

## 5. Fitness or wellness coach

> Strength coach for women over 40 who want to lift without wrecking their
> joints. 12-week program, no gym required. Free 3-move starter routine ↓

**What it's doing:** Names a specific audience (women over 40) instead of
"fitness for everyone," which filters for the right followers instead of
maximizing follower count with the wrong ones. The program detail (12 weeks,
no gym) answers the two questions a prospective client has before they even ask.

## 6. Artist or creative professional

> Illustrator. Editorial work for The Atlantic, Bloomberg, and Wired.
> Commissions open — link below.

**What it's doing:** Lets recognizable client names do the credibility work
instead of adjectives like "talented" or "passionate." "Commissions open"
is a clear commercial signal that turns admiration into inquiries.

## 7. Nonprofit or cause-driven account

> We place rescue dogs in homes across the Pacific Northwest. 1,200+ adoptions
> since 2015. See who's available now ↓

**What it's doing:** The number establishes scale and trust quickly. The CTA
("see who's available now") is specific and actionable, rather than a generic
"support our mission," which is harder to act on immediately.

## 8. Real estate agent

> Helping first-time buyers navigate the Denver market without the jargon.
> 60+ closings, average 11 days faster than market. Free buyer's guide ↓

**What it's doing:** Specializes in a buyer segment (first-time buyers) rather
than "all your real estate needs," which is the default and forgettable
framing most agents use. Two numbers — closings and speed — do more
convincing than any adjective could.

## 9. Musician or band

> Indie folk duo from Austin. New EP out now. Tour dates and tickets below ↓

**What it's doing:** States genre and location in the first sentence, which is
exactly what a new listener needs to decide if this is for them. The two
links (music, tickets) map to the two things a fan actually wants to do next.

## The pattern across all nine

Every bio above does three things: names a specific audience or niche instead
of a broad category, includes one piece of concrete proof (a number, a client
name, a fact), and ends with a clear next action. None of them try to
describe a full personality — they describe what the visitor gets by
following or clicking.

The mistake most bios make isn't being boring — it's being generic enough to
apply to a thousand other accounts in the same space. Specificity is what
actually converts, even though it feels like it narrows your audience.

Notice, too, that none of these bios try to say everything at once. A coffee
roastery doesn't mention its wholesale program in the bio; a real estate agent
doesn't list every certification. Pick the one line of proof and the one CTA
that matters most for the visitor you actually want, and cut the rest — it can
live in a pinned post or a link-in-bio page instead.
